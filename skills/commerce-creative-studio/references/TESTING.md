# Creative testing and learning

## Test card

Record test_id, store, product, variant, market, currency, objective, placement, audience,
start/end, attribution definition, hypothesis, manipulated variable, control, variant ids,
asset hashes, budget cap, minimum evidence and stop conditions. The operator sets the budget;
the agent prepares the test and later reads results. It never activates ads or changes spend.

## First batch

Begin with one approved product, one destination lane and one offer. Prepare three UGC
hook alternatives sharing one body, plus two banner concepts. Treat video-vs-image and
hook-vs-hook as different experiments. Rendering all possibilities first wastes review
capacity and makes it hard to explain results. Produce a pilot, review it, then expand.

## Interpretation

Track spend, impressions, clicks, landing views, checkouts, attributed purchases, gross and
net order revenue, refunds, replacements and contribution. Preserve exact denominators:
3-second-view/impressions is not the same as video-completion/video-starts. Keep attribution
windows, placements, reporting timezones and currencies separate. Missing tracking is
unknown, not zero sales. A small number of conversions does not justify declaring a winner.

Use the product's current target CPA and contribution model rather than a universal ROAS
threshold. CTR and hook rate diagnose attention; profit after variable costs decides whether
a campaign can be scaled. Validate supplier stock, delivery performance and support backlog
before suggesting higher spend. An ad may look profitable before returns mature.

## Feedback loop

High attention/low clicks -> check promise and CTA.
High clicks/low landing conversion -> check price, shipping, product match, speed and trust.
Conversions/high refunds -> inspect product fidelity, supplier quality and misleading expectations.
Rising frequency/worse response -> formulate a fatigue hypothesis, check audience/auction changes,
then change one angle or hook. Do not label every drop as fatigue.

The next brief should contain: observation, competing explanations, chosen next experiment,
evidence path, what stays fixed, what changes and who approves. Do not retroactively change
success criteria to make the previous idea appear to win.
